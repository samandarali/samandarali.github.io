Borrower and Financing Request

Maple Ridge Manufacturing Ltd. is an Ontario-based commercial packaging manufacturer owned and managed by its two founding shareholders. The company employs 38 people and has a reported satisfactory repayment history with its current lender.

The borrower is requesting a $750,000 five-year term loan to purchase automated production equipment and a $250,000 operating line of credit to support increased working-capital requirements.

The owners propose to contribute $150,000 toward the equipment purchase. Management expects the equipment to increase production capacity by 25% and reduce operating costs. These expected benefits have not yet been independently verified.

Revenue increased by approximately 23% between 2024 and 2026. However, this growth did not produce stronger earnings or cash flow. EBITDA margin declined from 12.71% to 10.17%, net income declined to $215,000 and operating cash flow declined to $325,000.

The current ratio declined from 1.65 to 1.37, indicating a weakening short-term financial cushion. Debt-to-equity increased from 0.69 to 0.82, indicating greater reliance on debt financing.

Operating cash flow divided by annual debt service is shown as a simplified coverage indicator. It should not be treated as the lender’s formal DSCR without confirming the applicable DSCR definition.

Repayment Capacity

The primary repayment source would be cash generated through normal business operations. In 2026, operating cash flow of $325,000 was only slightly higher than existing annual debt service of $320,000.

This represents simplified cash-flow coverage of approximately 1.02× and leaves little capacity to absorb weaker performance, unexpected expenses or additional debt payments.

A $750,000 loan amortized over five years would require at least $150,000 of annual principal repayment before interest. Based on current historical cash flow, the borrower does not demonstrate sufficient capacity to service the proposed additional term debt.

Repayment therefore depends heavily on the expected production growth and cost savings from the new equipment. These benefits should not be relied upon without supportable pro forma financial statements and sensitivity analysis.

Key Credit Strengths

  • Established 12-year operating history
  • Experienced owner-management team
  • Satisfactory reported repayment history
  • Consistent revenue growth
  • Continued positive EBITDA, net income and operating cash flow
  • Increasing shareholders’ equity
  • $150,000 proposed owner contribution

Key Credit Risks

  • Declining EBITDA margin, net income and operating cash flow
  • Weakening liquidity
  • Increasing leverage
  • Minimal historical debt-service cushion
  • Approximately 30% of revenue comes from one major customer whose contract expires in 10 months and has not been renewed.
  • Expected production increases and cost savings from the new equipment have not been independently verified.
  • The equipment’s estimated liquidation value of $500,000 represents approximately 66.7% of the requested term loan before realization costs.

Security and Guarantees

The proposed security consists of a general security agreement over business assets, a first-ranking security interest in the financed equipment, assignment of eligible accounts receivable and personal guarantees from both shareholders.

The equipment value, lien priority, receivable eligibility and guarantor financial strength remain subject to verification. The proposed collateral does not eliminate the weakness in primary repayment capacity.

Recommendation

Decline the $1,000,000 financing request as currently proposed.

Historical cash flow does not support the proposed additional debt. Revenue is increasing, but margins, net income, operating cash flow, liquidity and debt-service coverage are deteriorating while leverage is increasing.

A smaller or restructured facility may be reconsidered if additional analysis demonstrates acceptable repayment capacity. The term-loan amount should be determined by stressed cash-flow capacity rather than solely by the equipment purchase price. Any operating line should be limited by a borrowing base calculated from eligible receivables and, where appropriate, eligible inventory.

Requirements for Reconsideration

  1. Integrated projected income statements, balance sheets and cash-flow statements.
  2. A complete schedule of existing and proposed debt service.
  3. Acceptable DSCR under base-case and reasonable downside assumptions.
  4. Evidence supporting the projected production increase and cost savings.
  5. Confirmation of the major customer’s contract renewal or a credible customer-replacement plan.
  6. Current receivable, payable and inventory reports.
  7. A borrowing-base calculation supporting the operating-line amount.
  8. An independent equipment appraisal and confirmation of first-ranking security.
  9. Current personal financial statements and credit reports for both guarantors.
  10. Evidence that the owners’ $150,000 contribution will be invested before or alongside lender funding.
