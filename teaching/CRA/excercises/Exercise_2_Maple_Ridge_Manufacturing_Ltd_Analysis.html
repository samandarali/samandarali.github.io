Maple Ridge is asking the Bank to take over a relationship its current lender is exiting after a covenant breach, and at the same time to lend $1.5M for new equipment. That is a total request of $4.14M against normalized 2026 EBITDA of $590k. Revenue has grown 26% in two years, but EBITDA and margins have fallen, the cash conversion cycle has lengthened to 70 days, government remittances are overdue, and the owners have taken $180k of dividends and $180k of advances while profit fell.

As requested the credit does not work. Pro forma funded debt is 6.23x 2026 EBITDA. Repayment depends entirely on the new line and on Customer X renewing at current prices. In the lender downside, coverage after tax and maintenance CapEx is 0.75x, and loss of Customer X takes it to 0.57x. The $1.4M line is not supported by the borrowing base (bank-standard 0.82x), and the term debt is 67% covered by equipment liquidation values.

A smaller credit may work. Refinancing the existing equipment debt over six years with a $1.0M margined line costs about $327k a year — coverage of 1.81x on EBITDA and 1.11x after tax and CapEx on 2026 earnings, if dividends stop. We recommend declining the request as proposed and offering this counter-proposal, subject to Customer X renewing, the government arrears being paid and the shareholder advances being repaid before closing. The equipment loan can be reconsidered once the renewal is signed and with more equity.

Packaging for food producers is a steady market, but it is competitive and buyers are powerful. Margins depend on passing paperboard and resin costs through to customers, which Maple Ridge mostly cannot do (no price-adjustment clauses). Three structural issues dominate the business risk: concentration (Customer X is renegotiating for a 4% price cut), governance (related-party rent with no lease, advances to a shareholder, compiled statements and a part-time controller), and ownership transition (a $1.8M buyout of the majority owner planned from 2028).

What the sensitivities tell us

  • The requested structure needs management's plan to be met almost exactly. Lender DSCR reaches 1.0x only with EBITDA of about $901k ($239k below management's plan) and 1.25x at $1,131k — at management's own projection.
  • Customer X pricing matters a lot. Each 1% price cut on the renewal costs about $28k of EBITDA. A 4% cut alone removes $110k.
  • Exchange rate: a 10% weaker Canadian dollar adds about $170k to costs and $126k to the equipment price. The company has no hedging.
  • Losing Customer X drops lender DSCR to 0.57x. Repayment would then depend on collateral that covers only 67% of term debt.
  • The buyout: paying the majority owner $600k a year from the company would take 2028 lender DSCR to 0.68x even on management's numbers.
  • Structure: the counter-offer (refinance over 6 years plus a $1.0M line) is the only alternative covered on current earnings (1.11x). It still falls to 0.90x if Customer X's price cut hits before the equipment savings arrive, which is why the renewal is a condition.

11Primary and Secondary Repayment Sources

Primary – operating cash flow

Historical cash flow after tax, CapEx and dividends covered only 0.42x of 2026 debt service. For the requested structure, primary repayment depends on future savings and on Customer X renewing on acceptable terms. Assessment: inadequate as requested; marginal (1.11x) for the counter-offer.

Secondary – collateral and guarantees

  • Equipment liquidation value $1,830k covers 67% of $2.74M term debt (shortfall $910k).
  • Borrowing base $1,151k after priority reserves — less than the takeover balance plus growth needs.
  • Guarantees limited to $750k. Okafor's wealth is mainly his home and MRM Properties shares; Petrova's is ≈ $0.6M.
  • MRM Properties equity (≈ $1.5M) is the best untapped source but is not offered.

Assessment: insufficient without MRM Properties support.

13Incumbent Lender and Takeover Risk

Taking over a relationship that another bank is exiting carries adverse-selection risk. Lakeshore knows this borrower better than we do, has waived one breach, repriced the line and asked the company to leave. Management describes the move as looking for "a more supportive bank", which does not match what Lakeshore's letter says. The Bank should:

  • Obtain Lakeshore's covenant certificates and waiver letter, and the account conduct history (overdrafts, returned items, late payments).
  • Confirm there are no other defaults, including on the MRM Properties mortgage with the other lender.
  • Get CRA statements of account to confirm the exact HST and source-deduction arrears and any assessments.
  • Treat the takeover as new credit and apply full underwriting standards. Pricing should not be used to win the business.
