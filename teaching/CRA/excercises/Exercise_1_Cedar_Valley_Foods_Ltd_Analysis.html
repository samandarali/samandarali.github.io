Cedar Valley Foods is an established, 14-year borrower with an unblemished payment record and steadily growing demand. The weakness is not sales but conversion of sales into cash: EBITDA fell from $810k to $720k while revenue grew 16.1%, the cash conversion cycle stretched from 55 days to 62 days, and 2026 operating cash flow ($120k) did not cover unfinanced CapEx and distributions, producing negative free cash flow of $190k.

On the case's headline measure (EBITDA ÷ term debt service of $595k) coverage is adequate in every scenario — 1.21x on 2026 actual EBITDA, 1.82x on management's 2027 projection and 1.28x in the lender downside. After cash taxes, maintenance CapEx and operating-line interest, however, coverage is only 1.24x in the management case and 0.88x in the downside — and that is with distributions stopped. Repayment therefore depends on the new line delivering its labour savings, on the Customer A contract, and on distributions being suspended. Collateral leaves a $375k shortfall on the term loan, only partly covered by the $300k limited guarantees.

The request is supportable because the operating line is well covered by the borrowing base, the equipment is productive (not speculative), management is experienced, and the downside still shows EBITDA above term debt service. It is not supportable as requested: approval is conditional on a 7-year amortization, suspension of distributions, postponement of shareholder loans, staged draws against the installation, and tight reporting and covenants (Section 14).

The company sells frozen meals to grocery chains, independent supermarkets and institutional buyers. Demand has grown every year (revenue CAGR 7.7% over 2024–2026) and the company has never missed a scheduled loan payment. Priya Sharma has run the business for its whole 14-year history, supported by an experienced operations manager.

Business risk assessment: Frozen food is a non-discretionary, relatively recession-resistant category, but it is a low-margin business exposed to (i) raw-material and protein price swings, (ii) the buying power of large grocery chains, (iii) food-safety and recall risk, and (iv) capacity limits. Three customers account for 52% of revenue, and the largest (24%) can end its new contract for performance failures. That is high concentration for a borrower of this size. Key-person dependence on Priya Sharma, with no formal succession plan, is a further qualitative weakness.

Working-capital management is the main operating weakness. Between 2024 and 2026, A/R grew 41% and inventory 46%, against revenue growth of 16%. Shareholder loans – structural issue

The $150k shareholder loans are unsecured, interest-free and have no repayment terms, and the owners have not agreed to postpone them. As things stand they could be withdrawn at any time, ahead of the bank, out of the same thin cash flow. They count as debt — not as quasi-equity — until a postponement and subordination agreement is signed. With postponement, adjusted equity becomes $2,560k and pro forma TL/adjusted equity improves to 1.85x.

Equity does not reconcile

Opening equity + net income − distributions does not equal reported equity (table below): equity is $222k lower than expected in 2025 and $98k higher in 2026. Possible explanations are share redemptions, dividends recorded separately from "distributions", prior-period adjustments or a capital injection. The accountant must explain this before funding, because it affects tangible net worth and the reliability of the statements.

What the sensitivities tell us

  • EBITDA-basis coverage holds up well. Coverage stays above 1.0x unless revenue misses plan by more than about 18% with half the savings, and stays above 1.25x down to roughly a 11% miss — the exercise's downside sits inside that range.
  • Lender-basis coverage is fragile. To reach 1.25x after tax and maintenance CapEx the company needs about $1,093k of EBITDA — above management's own plan. Even 1.0x needs ~$867k: revenue within ~11% of plan with full savings, or within ~5% with half the savings.
  • Gross margin is the biggest single risk. Each 1-point drop in gross margin removes ~$115k of EBITDA — almost half the labour saving. A 2-point drop, about the same as 2024–2026, cuts management-case EBITDA to $850k and its lender DSCR to 0.98x.
  • Interest-rate risk is small because the term debt is fixed; +300 bp on prime costs only $33k a year.
  • Customer concentration is the tail risk. Losing Customer A brings EBITDA down to about $600k — 1.01x on term debt service and below 1.0x on a lender basis — so the loan would rely on collateral and guarantees.
  • Structure helps somewhat. Moving to a 7-year amortization (the equipment's useful life is well over 7 years) lowers annual service by $29.1k; adding 4 months interest-only during installation improves first-year coverage further. Neither fixes a downside shortfall on its own, so they must be combined with suspended distributions and close monitoring.

13Risk Mitigants

  • Proven track record: 14 years of operations, the same leadership throughout, and no missed loan payments.
  • Productive, self-funding asset: labour savings alone repay the project in ~6.2 years; the investment directly addresses the cost problem that has been squeezing margins.
  • Meaningful owner equity: $300k (20% of cost) contributed in cash; book equity of $2,410k.

  • Coverage holds on EBITDA: EBITDA covers term debt service 1.28x even in the lender downside.
  • Line well margined: borrowing base 1.45x the limit; monthly margining limits the bank's exposure to the working-capital cycle.

14Recommended Structure and Conditions

Term loan – $1,200,000

  • Amortization extended to 7 years (payment ≈ $222,664/yr), within the equipment's useful life; interest-only during the 4-month installation, then blended payments.
  • Advanced in progress draws against vendor invoices and confirmation of installation, with a 10% holdback until commissioning and a performance test. The $300k equity goes in first.
  • First-ranking PMSI charge on the new equipment; cross-collateralized and cross-defaulted with the operating line and existing facilities.

Operating line – $1,100,000

  • Approved at the requested limit, available only up to the borrowing base: 75% of eligible A/R < 90 days, plus 50% of eligible finished goods and raw materials (excluding WIP and slow-moving stock), with inventory capped at $550k.
  • Exclusions: A/R over 90 days, disputed and contra accounts, cross-aged debtors (more than 50% over 90 days), single-debtor concentration above 30%; reserves for priority payables.
  • Monthly borrowing-base certificate with aged A/R and A/P listings within 20 days of month-end.

Conditions precedent

  1. Signed postponement and subordination agreement for the $150k shareholder loans (no repayment without the bank's written consent).
  2. Proof that the $300k equity contribution has been injected; signed equipment purchase contract, installation schedule and vendor warranty.
  3. Satisfactory written explanation of the equity reconciliation gaps, and review-engagement (or audited) year-end statements.
  4. Copy of the Customer A contract reviewed by the bank (termination clauses, performance standards, pricing and raw-material pass-through terms).
  5. Written resolution of the $85k disputed receivable (or confirmation that it is excluded from the base); inventory field exam before first advance under the increased limit.
  6. Joint and several guarantees of the owners (recommended increase to $500k, or unlimited for Priya Sharma), with current personal net worth statements.
  7. Assignment of insurance with the bank as first loss payee; business-interruption cover for the installation period; key-person life insurance on Priya Sharma (≥ $500k) assigned to the bank.

Financial covenants (tested annually on year-end statements; quarterly reporting)

  • Fixed-charge coverage ≥ 1.20x — (EBITDA − cash taxes − unfinanced CapEx − distributions) ÷ (term principal + interest); tested from FY2027.
  • Funded debt / EBITDA ≤ 4.25x at FY2027, stepping down to ≤ 3.50x at FY2028 (shareholder loans excluded once postponed).
  • Current ratio ≥ 1.20x; TL / (tangible net worth + postponed SH loans) ≤ 2.25x.
  • Distributions: none until FY2027 audited results show fixed-charge coverage ≥ 1.25x after the proposed distribution; thereafter capped at 50% of prior-year net income.
  • CapEx above $250k a year (excluding this project) requires the bank's consent; no additional debt or liens without consent.

Monitoring

  • Quarterly internal statements with covenant calculations; monthly reports on installation progress until commissioning.
  • Six-month review against the projections: actual labour savings, gross margin and Customer A volumes. Material variance triggers a structure review.
  • Annual field exam of receivables and inventory; written succession plan within 12 months.

15Final Recommendation

APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS

We recommend approving the $1,200,000 term loan and the increase of the operating line to $1,100,000, subject to the structure and conditions in Section 14.

Why not a straight approval: Historical cash flow does not support the new debt. In 2026, coverage of existing debt service after tax, CapEx and distributions was 0.87x, and free cash flow was negative $190k. Pro forma leverage of 4.88x on 2026 EBITDA is high, margins and working-capital quality are deteriorating, and the term loan is under-secured by $375k. As requested — 6-year amortization, distributions continuing and shareholder loans not postponed — the downside lender DSCR falls to 0.66x, which is not acceptable.

Why not a decline: The project directly fixes the cost problem behind the weak results, and the labour saving is largely within management's control. EBITDA covers term debt service even in the lender downside (1.28x). The operating line is well covered by the borrowing base (1.45x). The borrower has a 14-year clean repayment record and experienced management. The key risks can be reduced through structure: a longer amortization and interest-only period lower annual service, suspending distributions keeps ~$150k a year in the business, postponement locks in the shareholder loans, staged draws protect against installation failure, and covenants and monthly margining give early warning.

With these conditions, first-year lender DSCR (7-year amortization with interest-only during installation) improves to about 1.39x in the management case and 0.99x in the downside; at steady state on the 7-year schedule it is 1.29x and 0.92x. The remaining downside gap (~$50k a year) can be absorbed by line availability of about $420k, while the bank monitors closely. If FY2027 results come in below the downside case, the bank should require an equity injection or a reduction in the line. Proposed risk rating: 6 (Satisfactory – Watch), to be reviewed after the first six months of operating the new line.
